    Website of The Day

Likova is a Class A business center: stepped volumes of glass and metal at varying heights, lifted off the ground, a green courtyard and a viewing platform at its heart. The architecture is built around one conviction — a workplace should inspire. A presence like that demands a digital statement built from the same source, where every decision traces back to the building itself.

This is what commercial real estate website design looks like when every choice follows one logic: rich storytelling, animation tuned to the rhythm of the building's form, a WebGL scene with an interactive 3D model, and an interactive site plan. Together they create a digital experience that could only belong to this project.

Energy captured in glass

The visual language starts with a single object: an abstract 3D form that carries the energy of the place. Offset glass volumes arranged into a cubic shape mirror the stepped logic of the architecture, while refracted light gives it depth and presence. Glass is a right metaphor here — transparency, clarity, and a direct echo of the building's own glazed facades.

Architecture in motion

The building's key characteristics come through three interactive WebGL scenes, each with a distinct job. The 3D model is the one you explore: rotate it, and information about its architecture and specifications surfaces as you go. The interactive site plan turns the layout into something to move through. That's what makes this an interactive 3D website rather than a gallery — every angle and every detail arrives at whatever pace the visitor chooses.



That's the difference between a render and 3D on a site. A render is a picture: one angle, one light, one moment. 3D is an experience: the visitor explores the building, sees it in context, and moves through the information at their own pace.

Recognisable restraint

Likova's visual signature comes from a single architectural feature: the stepped structure — planes offset from one another, the building caught mid-shift. That principle became the recurring motif of the interface. As the user scrolls, stepped panels flow into each other and reshape, as if the building were quietly rearranging itself in real time.

Interactive site plan

The site plan isn't a diagram — it's a 3D environment built in WebGL that you move through rather than read. It works like an interactive property map. Rotate the model and the territory reveals itself: where the buildings sit, what zones exist, how everything around them is arranged. A model you can move through communicates place in a way a flat plan simply can't.

The materiality of interiors

The interior finishes are shown through custom 3D visualizations we built in-house , layered with a parallax effect that moves the scene at different speeds as the user scrolls. The result is depth — another dimension added to the experience, so that exploring the space feels complete rather than sampled.

Two ways to find an office

The site offers two ways to find an office. A visual office selector for those who browse by feel — choosing space the way they'd walk a floor. A parametric one for those who know exactly what they need — filtering by area, floor, and layout. Two search modes for two mindsets.

The result

Likova was named Site of the Day at Awwwards — recognition from the international web design community for work on a Class A business center. Creative design with interactive 3D and storytelling is still the exception in commercial real estate, not the norm. It's the standard our real estate website design agency holds itself to. And as a piece of commercial real estate website design, the site continues the thought the architecture began: a workplace should inspire.
